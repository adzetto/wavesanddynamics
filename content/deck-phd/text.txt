Problem Statement | Federal Railway Administration’s safety report demonstrates that, among all rail defects, transverse defects located inside the railhead (see Figure 1) are listed as one of the main causes of railway track-related incidents.11.2% (2789 out of 24777 accidents) of railway track-related train accidents between 1991 and 2020 were caused by transverse defects. A major accident occurred in Columbus in 2011 due to transverse defects is shown in Figure 2.Such accidents cost $655 Million in damages in the US in the last 2 decades.  | Figure 1 |  | Railhead | Transverse Crack | Figure 2 | 
Existing technology to identify rail defects is based on ultrasonic sensors placed on hang-pushed carts, hi-rail trucks, or specific rail cars.  These systems can not perform defect identification reliably at high speeds and they are not mounted on operating train cars. They are also bigger than our system and involve more instrumentation (link as an example). | Current solution or alternatives | 
We discovered that (i) two laser doppler vibrometers (LDVs) can be placed in front of an operating train (see Figure 1) to record the waves propagating in the railhead, and (ii) the change in these waves due to a transverse crack can be used for the identification of the crack (Such system is also our intellectual property: Link)Accordingly, we developed a damage detection system that incorporates two LDVs.The system has a small size and can be connected to any operating train through a holder incorporating vibration isolators (See Figure 2.
