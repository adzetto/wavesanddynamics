Poisson processes (1): counting random events

Example: trucks arrive at a weigh station at random, 2 per hour on average (rate 𝜆 = 2 per hour). Arrivals are independent and the rate stays constant.

1 Each tick = one truck arriving (3 different days)

One realization = one possible day. The rate is an average: first 2 h had 2, 3 and 2 trucks; 10 h had 24, 13 and 26 (expected 20).

2 Count so far: each step up = one truck

𝑁(𝑡) = trucks so far. Each truck adds a step of 1 at a random time, so every day draws a different staircase around 𝜆𝑡.

𝜆 fixes the average; randomness decides the actual arrival times and counts.
