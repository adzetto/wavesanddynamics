# gallery: his photographs (gallery.html)

The live Wix gallery, pulled by tools/wix_pull.py into content/gallery/ (manifest.json: 14 sets, 26 photos, 1 film). The page shows 12 sets: the second set repeated three times on Wix (the same three photos, "at TTC" then twice "at TTCI") appears once, with its first caption. His captions are the set headings (h2, serif 600 21px), his "(during my ...)" line one step quieter; no photo is cropped and none has a caption of its own.
Rows: each set is one row at one height (flex widths from each photo's proportions, --ar over the row's sum --r), filling the text column unless the row would pass --hmax (380px, 330px under 1200px), where it stops short. Under 640px the row breaks into justified pairs; a pair whose proportions sum above 3.0 becomes two single rows. Both layouts are computed at build time; nothing moves after paint.
Files (build.py derive(), cached in build/webp): a thumbnail at 800px long edge (q72) and a full file at 2048px (q80), EXIF orientation applied first, sRGB, no EXIF/GPS/ICC in the output. --strict fails on any published picture that still carries metadata.
Viewer: a modal <dialog> made by the script; without JavaScript each link opens the full file. Opens by growing from the thumbnail (340ms, --ease), closes back to the thumbnail of the photo on screen (260ms) or fades when that thumbnail is off screen. Arrow keys, Home, End, and the buttons step through the whole gallery (instant, a 120ms fade); a touch swipe tracks 1:1, commits past a quarter of the stage or above .35px/ms, rubber-bands at the ends; a swipe down closes, the photo shrinking about its centre and landing on its thumbnail from wherever the pull left it. A finger never waits for an animation: one that lands on a moving photo (thrown, coming in, springing back) stops it where it is on screen and drags it on from there; a thrown photo, or one still growing out of its thumbnail, lands at once. Focus goes to Close on open and back to the current photo's thumbnail on close. Reduced motion: opacity fades only. Pinch zoom hands panning back to the browser.
The film: a poster tile with a play mark and its length. The stream stays on his Wix media (720p rendition) and loads only when played; Wix's static host rejects video. A YouTube upload by him would be more durable (see the module docstring). It has sound, so the player gets captions (`FILM_CAPTIONS`, a WebVTT file made in the page, off until turned on): "[Faint voices]" for its first six seconds, then the truck coming up the track. A speech recogniser hears a few words in those voices, not surely enough to publish; someone who listens can write them in.
