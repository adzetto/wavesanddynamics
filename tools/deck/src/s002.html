THE FULL WORKFLOW OF A PROBABILISTIC DECISION (FROM STATISTICS TO PROBABILISTIC DECISION – ALL STEPS INVOLVE PROBABILITY)

Five stages carry data to a decision. The first 3 are optional, sometimes the model is already known, and you can skip straight to using it.Probabilistic models (that we will cover in this class) are distribution and regression based.

DATA
1

GOODNESS FIT

checks whether the family you assumed is a plausible fit for the data

chi square test

compares observed counts to what the assumed family predicts

probability plots

points should fall near a line if the family fits well

also used: Kolmogorov Smirnov, Anderson Darling, Shapiro Wilk

2

ESTIMATION

a best fit number from a sample, on its own just a point, not yet a probability

Probability distributions’ parameters are estimated (mean, variance, etc)

correlation and regression

covariance and r show the linkage, least squares fits the line

3

INFERENCE

attaches a probability to that estimate: how sure, and how wide

confidence and hypothesis tests

a plausible range, and is an assumed value still ok

regression inference

is the slope real, is the fit valid, how wide is the prediction

STATISTICS SECTION: OPTIONAL, DEPENDING ON THE PROBLEM

1

checking an assumed model against fresh data

2

a quick point value, family and confidence not needed

12

the family is unsure, so test it, then fit it

23

the family is trusted, but confidence is still needed

123

the full rigorous path, from family, to fit, to confidence

None

Just a model assumed (to be right)

4

PROBABILITY MODELS

the rules for combining and describing random outcomes

counting rules and Bayes

count outcomes, combine chances, update them with new evidence

random variables and distributions

normal, lognormal, binomial, …, each with its own mean and spread

RISK AND RELIABILITY

turns the model into the chance of a real engineering outcome

uncertainty propagation

carry input randomness (and its covariance) through a formula

reliability (FOSM)

chance that capacity falls short of demand

5

DECISION

Reading probabilities 2 different way is a separate choice

Point Wise Decision

REGRESSION

the predicted number, used directly

RELIABILITY (FOSM)

the mean, or a single chosen quantile

RELIABILITY (FOSM)

pass or fail against a safety index

Probabilistic Decision

REGRESSION

the chance the true value exceeds a limit

RELIABILITY (FOSM)

the chance of exceeding that value

RELIABILITY (FOSM)

the actual chance capacity falls short
