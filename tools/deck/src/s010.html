One core idea: every model is a probability distribution

Yes: at the core there is one model, the probability distribution. Regression, Bayesian models, processes and state-space models are distributions of different things.

p(x)

One quantity

Distribution: Concrete strength

p(x, y)

Several together

Joint distribution: Load and capacity

p(y | x)

y given predictors x

Regression: Strength given w/c ratio

p(θ | data)

Unknown parameters

Bayesian model: Mean strength after 25 tests

p(x₁, x₂, …, xₜ)

A whole history

Stochastic process: Traffic over one day

p(xₜ | z₁ … zₜ)

Hidden state from sensors

Kalman filter: Displacement now

So distributions and regression are not two separate kinds of model: regression is a distribution whose mean moves with x. The rest add more variables, uncertain parameters, time or hidden states.

Every probabilistic model answers one question: how is probability spread, and over what?
