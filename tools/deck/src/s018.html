Bayesian updating: deriving the posterior

We describe uncertainty about the unknown mean μ with a prior, then update it using the observed tests.

Ingredients and notation

D: the observed strength measurementsPrior: μ ∼ N(m₀, v₀)m₀: prior mean; v₀: prior variancevᴅ = σ²/n: variance of the sample mean

Bayes’ rule

p(μ | D) ∝ p(D | μ) × p(μ)

posterior ∝ likelihood × prior

∝ means “proportional to.” A normalizing constant makes the total probability equal to one.

Combine the two squared distances

p(μ | D) ∝ exp{−½[(μ − m₀)²/v₀

p(μ | D) ∝ exp{−½[+ (μ − x̄)²/vᴅ]}

Expand the squares and collect terms in μ:

μ²(1/v₀ + 1/vᴅ) − 2μ(m₀/v₀ + x̄/vᴅ)

Then complete the square: (μ − mₚ)²/vₚ.

vₚ = 1 / (1/v₀ + 1/vᴅ)

mₚ = vₚ(m₀/v₀ + x̄/vᴅ)

mₚ = posterior mean; vₚ = posterior variance.

Multiplying two normal-shaped functions adds their precisions: precision = 1 / variance.
