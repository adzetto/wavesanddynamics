Where stationarity helps, and where it does not

Many methods summarize a stable window. Others explicitly model trends, changing variability or transient events.

Vibration / Welch PSD

Pool segments when their second-order behavior is reasonably stable.

Speech / short-time spectra

Analyze short frames so changing sounds remain visible.

Fatigue / spectral methods

Stationary stress models support spectral approximations. Rainflow also handles variable histories.

Econometrics / ARIMA and ADF

Differencing can remove a unit root. ADF tests for a unit root, not every form of nonstationarity.

EEG / windowed band power

Compare short epochs rather than blur different brain states into one spectrum.

Process control / Shewhart, CUSUM

Compare with a stable baseline. Serial dependence can require adjusted chart limits.

Stationarity is a modeling assumption to assess, not a prerequisite for every time-series method.
