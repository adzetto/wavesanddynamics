From time to frequency: correlation gives the spectrum

Three views of the same random vibration. The spectrum is the Fourier transform of the autocorrelation, so the two carry the same information, arranged differently.

1 Variance

σ² = E[x²] for zero mean. The signal was divided by its SD, so σ² = 1.

2 Autocorrelation

R(τ) = E[x(t)·x(t+τ)]: compare x with a copy shifted by τ. R(0) = σ².

3 Spectrum

S(f) = ∫ R(τ) e^(−i2πfτ) d𝜏. Area: ∫ G(f) df = σ² = 1.

The area under the spectrum is the variance: the spectrum shows how the variance is shared among frequencies.
