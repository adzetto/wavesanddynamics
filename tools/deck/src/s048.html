Estimating a spectrum (1): the FFT and the periodogram

Slide 47 used the true R(τ). A finite record only gives an estimate. The FFT is the calculator; the periodogram is the estimate built from it.

Why so jagged?

Each value rests on a single Fourier coefficient, so its scatter is about as large as the value. A longer record gives more points, not smoother ones.

Three steps

1 FFT: X(f) = Σ x[n] e^(−i2πfnΔt)

A fast way to get the Fourier coefficients: complex, with amplitude and phase.

2 Periodogram: P(f) = (Δt/N)·|X(f)|²

Square the magnitude, scale to x² per Hz, drop the phase.

3 Link to slide 47

P(f) is the Fourier transform of the sample autocorrelation: the same time-to-frequency link, applied to data.

The FFT computes; the periodogram estimates. One periodogram is right on average but very jagged.
