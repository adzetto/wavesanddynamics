MATERIAL AND METHOD
  steel: E = 210 GPa, nu = 0.29, rho = 7850 kg/m^3: c_L = 5920.9 m/s, c_T = 3220.1 m/s,
  c_0 = sqrt(E/rho) = 5172.2 m/s, c_R = 2981.3 m/s (the Rayleigh equation's root, c_R/c_T = 0.92584)
  SAFE: u = U(y, z) exp(i(kx - wt)); 9-node isoparametric quadrilaterals, 3 x 3 Gauss, 3 dof a node;
  [K1 + ik K2 + k^2 K3 - w^2 M] U = 0, made real symmetric by U_x = i V_x; each symmetry class on its
  own orthonormal basis; the lowest eigenpairs at each real k (dense eigh, or sparse shift-invert).
  The square bar, the pipe and the plate are meshed on structured grids; the I-beam and the rail by a
  constrained quality Delaunay triangulation of the outline (Shewchuk's Triangle, minimum angle 30
  degrees), each triangle split into three quadrilaterals, then 9-node elements, the boundary nodes moved
  onto the true outline; a half or quarter meshed and mirrored, so the mirror planes hold exactly.
  The window of every section: f up to its own fmax and c_p up to 10 km/s. fmax is chosen in a gap
  between the frequencies where branches enter the window (their c_p falling through its top), so that every
  branch drawn enters well before fmax and none is about to (each is drawn once). A branch that cuts on below
  fmax but enters the window only above it is not drawn: bar 161.01, 165.35, 180.01 kHz; I-beam 45.77 kHz.
  The cut-on marks on the top frame are the k = 0 frequencies of exactly the drawn higher order branches.

THE PAGE SOLVES THE WAVES IT SHOWS
  Each section travels as its mesh (nodes in float32, the Python model reading the same rounded
  values) and its symmetry. The page builds the same isoparametric Q9 elements (3 x 3 Gauss),
  assembles each class on its own basis (the projections of each orbit's displacements, orthonormalised:
  mirror planes, the square's C4v, or the pipe's harmonics) as a skyline in an RCM order, and solves a
  wave (class, branch, k) exactly: a skyline LDL^T of K(k) - s M counts its negative pivots (Sylvester:
  the eigenvalues below s), s moves until exactly `branch` lie below, and inverse iteration converges on
  the branch's own eigenpair; should it head for a nearer one below s, the count brackets the branch's
  alone, bisection narrows the bracket, and the iteration runs again from its middle.
  The bar's general assembly gives safe_model's rectangle matrices: max relative difference 2.1e-15.
  The page's script itself, under node, against scipy on the same mesh, at three random
  wavenumbers on every branch inside the window:

PERFORMANCE
  The page opens without solving: the bar's first stop (both waves: their eigenvectors, frequencies and
  group velocities) is solved at build time by the page's own script under node and carried in DATA,
  exactly what the page would solve. Everything else is done in small steps (about a millisecond each)
  while the page is idle (requestIdleCallback): every other section read, its elements' matrices and the
  classes of its first stop assembled and that stop's two waves solved, so that choosing it is quick; and
  the tour's states a stop ahead (wave 1, wave 2's keyframes). A frame that leaves time runs some of it too
  (on a frame under 6 ms, up to 4 ms of solving steps; else a millisecond of the tour's states): headless
  Chromium gives no idle time while the figure plays, so the queue (about half a second of work in all) would
  otherwise drain only by requestIdleCallback's timeout, 3 ms at a time (measured here at 1280 px: empty 2.5 s
  after the page opens on the bar, against 11.5 s so). The element matrices and the assembly lost their
  allocations (the same sums in the same order: the same matrices to the bit; 4 to 5 times quicker). Once
  the intro is over, the still parts (panel (a)'s frame, ticks, labels, shading and curves, the legend, the
  colour scale and the parameter lines) are drawn once and kept as a picture of the frame's pixels, laid
  down at each frame before what moves. The waveguides' faces are filled through a pattern of their colour
  image rather than drawn as parts of it (the same pixels, within 3 of 255; a tenth of the cost), only the
  columns of that image the packet covers are written each frame, and the lines along a waveguide are one
  segment where the packet is not (it is straight there).

GROUP VELOCITY
  Every wave's c_g is the page's own, from the eigenpair it solves: differentiating
  [K1 + k K2 + k^2 K3 - w^2 M] V = 0 in k and taking V^T of it, the terms in dV/dk cancel (K and M
  symmetric), leaving c_g = dw/dk = V^T (K2 + 2k K3) V / (2 w V^T M V) (Hellmann-Feynman): the exact
  slope of the model's own dispersion curve at that point, two skyline products per state.

THE PLATE AS A PLATE
  The plate's waves run along a wide, thin slab in an oblique view seen from above (its depth drawn at
  0.5 of the width, at 30 degrees): 10 mm thick and 140 mm wide, the slice's width drawn 3.5 times over (the plate
  is uniform across its width, so the slice's mode shape is the plate's). Its top face is lit by |u| at the
  surface, the same across the width: the crests are straight lines across the plate, for Lamb waves
  (u_x, u_z: the top face and the near edge heave) and SH waves (u_y: the top face's lines along x sway
  across). The section view above it shows the computed shape through the thickness.

INTERACTION
  The section switcher over (b) (a radio group: arrows move and choose) changes the window: the old
  frequency ticks fade before the new arrive, the fundamental curves morph at the same share of either
  window, the higher order curves cross fade, the markers glide and the waves fade over (0.8 s of wall
  time). A wave keeps its kind (bending, axial, torsional) at the same share of the new window, or takes
  the curve nearest in c_p; its packet keeps its share of the drawn length. The tour starts again on
  the new section. Hover names a curve and gives f, wavelength, c_p and c_g there; a marker or its thumb
  drags along its curve (on a backward-wave branch, to the crossing nearest the wave), the arrows step
  it (1 kHz on a 200 kHz window, in proportion on the others), up and down move it to the next curve.
  Once a reader takes over, a chip 'resume tour' over the plot hands back to the tour. Hit areas in the guide
  (672 px): a section's cell 48.7 x 29.6 px (its stand-in 6 units taller than the drawn cell above and below),
  a marker 24 x 24 px, the chip 79 x 28 px. Nothing to play or pause is drawn on the figure: the frame's own
  buttons (in the guide, the row under it) and Space do that, and a click on a control never pauses.
