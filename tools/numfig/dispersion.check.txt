THE BAR (safe_model.py; the section the page opens with)

MODEL
  steel bar, b x h = 20 x 40 mm (y x z), E = 210 GPa, nu = 0.29, rho = 7850 kg/m^3
  c_L = 5920.9 m/s, c_T = 3220.1 m/s, c_0 = sqrt(E/rho) = 5172.2 m/s
  c_R = 2981.3 m/s (root of the Rayleigh equation, c_R/c_T = 0.92584; Viktorov's (0.87 + 1.12 nu)/(1 + nu) gives 0.92620)
  SAFE: u = U(y, z) exp(i(kx - wt)); Q9 quadratic quadrilaterals, 3 dof per node,
  3 x 3 Gauss (exact for rectangles); [K1 + ik K2 + k^2 K3 - w^2 M] U = 0 made real
  symmetric by U_x = i V_x; sparse shift-invert eigenpairs at each real k, checked against dense eigh. The two mirror planes
  split the problem into four symmetry classes (orthonormal symmetry-adapted bases):
  (++) axial, (+-) vertical bending, (-+) lateral bending, (--) torsional.
  K2 antisymmetry check: max|K2 + K2^T|/max|K2| = 6.5e-17

THE PAGE SOLVES EVERY WAVE IT SHOWS
  Each section travels as its mesh (nodes in float32, the Python model reading the same rounded
  values) and its symmetry. The page builds the same isoparametric Q9 elements (3 x 3 Gauss),
  assembles each class on its own basis (mirror orbits, or the pipe's harmonics) as a skyline in
  an RCM order, and solves a wave (class, branch, k) exactly: the shift s sits 1e-4 of w under
  the drawn curve's w at k; a skyline LDL^T of K(k) - s M counts its negative pivots (Sylvester:
  the eigenvalues below s), s moves until exactly `branch` lie below, and inverse iteration
  converges on the branch's own eigenpair at that k. Should it head for a nearer eigenvalue
  below s, the count brackets the branch's alone, bisection narrows the bracket, and the
  iteration runs again from its middle.
  The bar's general assembly gives safe_model's matrices: max relative difference 3.8e-15.

  [K1 + k K2 + k^2 K3 - w^2 M] V = 0 in k and taking V^T of it, the terms in dV/dk cancel (K and M
  symmetric), leaving c_g = dw/dk = V^T (K2 + 2k K3) V / (2 w V^T M V) (Hellmann-Feynman): the exact
  slope of the model's own dispersion curve at that point, two skyline products per state.

TIME AND DRAWING
  The waves run as packets (b): each beam carries bursts of its own mode under a Hann envelope
  E(x) = cos^2(pi x / 160 mm) (160 mm across, 22.6 mm rms), one after another 572 mm apart,
  so one crosses the drawn 412 mm at a time, launched from the left end:
  u = E(x - X) Re{U exp(i(k(x - X) + psi))}. X, the envelope's centre, moves at c_g; psi, the phase
  at the centre, turns at k c_g - w, so a crest (k (x - X) + psi a whole turn) moves at
  X' - psi'/k = w/k = c_p. Both waves are on one clock, not written on the figure:
  2.511e+04 times slower than the beam's, so that a packet at the rod speed c_0 crosses the 412 mm
  in 2 s (1 s of the page is 39.8 us of the beam). The fastest c_g in any window is
  5.404 km/s (the plate's S0 Lamb at 0 kHz: 1.91 s); at the tours' states
  the packets cross in 1.9 to 9.0 s.
  A wave cycles on the page at f / 2.511e+04: 6.0 Hz at 150 kHz, 0.8 Hz at 20 kHz.
  In the tour X and psi are the exact c_g and k c_g - w integrated in time (the glides by the rule
  above), a pure function of the clock; under the reader's hand each frame moves them on by its
  time at the rates of the state then shown, so a new frequency changes the speed at once and the
  crests close in or open out about the centre, neither the envelope nor the phase at it jumping.
  Each section view shows its wave's cross-section at the packet's centre, u = Re{U exp(i psi)}:
  still for a wave that does not disperse (c_g = c_p), turning over for one that does.
  Tour: 16 s on the caption's comparison, then 18 s a stop: markers arrive, wave 2 glides up its
  curve over 8 s, a hold, 1 s of rest between modes; each stop launches both packets anew.
  Colour: |u| / U_rms at each point at that instant, E sqrt(U_x^2 sin^2 + (U_y^2 + U_z^2) cos^2) of
  the phase, U_rms the section's rms displacement at the packet's centre (U^T M U = rho A U_rms^2);
  0 to 3, so the beam is white where no packet is. Shape: the largest displacement drawn 18 units
  in the sections (each section at its own scale, the bar 7.5 units per mm) and at most 11 units
  along the beam, never steeper than 0.5 (2.2 units per mm, 412 mm ending at x = 0). Along the beam
  the boundary is cut into faces (runs of one direction); those turned to the reader are drawn far
  to near, each an image of |u| made each frame (a column a millimetre) laid on its deformed outline
  in pieces, fine over the packet and one piece over the still beam either side of it; the face at
  x = 0 last; creases and silhouettes are inked.

INTERACTION
  The section switcher over (b) (a radio group: arrows move and choose) morphs the fundamental
  curves into the new section's in 0.8 s of wall time (so also while the waves are paused), cross
  fades the higher order curves, glides the markers and fades the waves over. A wave keeps its
  kind (bending, axial, torsional) and frequency where the new section has that mode there, and
  otherwise takes the curve nearest in c_p. The tour starts again on the new section.
  The play control by the colour scale, and Space, pause and resume the waves: the phases stop
  with the clock and run on from there; dragging while paused redraws the frozen frame.
  The first touch of a curve, marker, thumb or key stops the tour; "resume tour" hands back
  after a 0.35 s fade, at the tour's next stop. Hover shows a curve's name and its f, wavelength,
  c_p and c_g (solved there) under the pointer. Each wave's label over its beam gives c_p, c_g and
  the wavelength; its slider reads them out. Hits: 18 units (marker), 12 (curve) with a mouse; 30 and 24 by
  touch. A drag follows its own curve, and moves to another only when the pointer is drawn
  well away (28 units) onto it (8 units).
