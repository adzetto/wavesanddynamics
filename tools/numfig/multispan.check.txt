nf-multispan: Figure 7, vibrational modes of two span beams

MODEL
  A partially continuous two span Euler-Bernoulli girder: pins at the three
  supports (w = 0; moment free at the two ends); over the middle pin the two
  spans keep their own end rotation, joined by a rotational spring k. Exact: the
  dynamic stiffness of the four support rotations from the closed form span
  transfer matrix plus the spring; natural frequencies by the Wittrick-Williams
  count (bisection to 1e-13; the spring has no frequencies of its own, so J0 is
  the spans' clamped-clamped count); mode shapes from the null vector. No mesh.
  One uniform girder, one frequency scale: EI = 1.0e+11 N m^2, m = 10000 kg/m, EI/m = 1.0e+07 m^4/s^2

FIT (python multispan.py --fit)
  His values are 'approximate frequency values from experience'; each span obeys
  f2 = 4 f1 to within 2 % (a: 1.3 -> 5.2, 1.45 -> 5.8 vs his 5.92; b: 1.23 -> 4.92
  vs 4.95, 5.8 -> 23.2 vs 22.90), which is two nearly independent simply supported
  spans: a weak joint. Free: the two span lengths and k (EI/m fixed; only c/L^2
  and k L/EI enter). For each k the lengths are refitted to his four values
  (minimax); k is then the stiffest joint for which every residual is within 2 %.
  Least squares alone takes (a) to k = 0 (two independent spans), because his
  5.92/1.45 = 4.08 exceeds the simply supported 4 and any joint only lowers it.
  Per span sections (right span EI and m both x r, r = 0.25 to 4) were tried: at the
  stiffest joint within 2 % some row still moves the other span only 1 to 5 % (a) and
  0.5 to 2.4 % (b), the 22.90 Hz row about 2 % for every r; no clearer, so one girder.
  Rounded: lengths to 0.1 m, k to two figures (down); residuals rechecked below.
