MODEL (his page iterative-solvers-animation.html, the same numbers)
  Figure 4's problem (sp_inv_lib; see sp_inv_reg.check.txt): N = 64, Gaussian blur s = 0.045, m a bump and a
  block; d = Gm + e with noise draw 1 (numpy default_rng seed 1) at 0.1, 1 and 10 % of max Gm; delta = sigma sqrt(N).
  Landweber: m_k = m_(k-1) + omega G^T (d - G m_(k-1)), m_0 = 0, omega = 1/s_1^2 = 1.0181743004; in closed form
  m_k = sum f_i (u_i^T d / s_i) v_i, f_i = 1 - (1 - omega s_i^2)^k (computed as -expm1(k log1p(-omega s_i^2))).
  CGLS: 50 iterations from m_0 = 0 (Hestenes-Stiefel form, no reorthogonalization), run for real.
  Effective filter of an iterate: f_i = s_i (v_i^T m_k) / (u_i^T d).

GRID
  Landweber's curves pass through 699 integer iterates: every k to 100, then 600 log-spaced k (rounded) to
  10^8; the iterate the reader picks (any integer k) is the page's own sum, its marker exactly on the curve's
  formula. The discrepancy stop is the first integer k with |G m_k - d| <= delta (bisection over integers); the
  best k minimizes the true error over every k to 5,000, then k growing by 0.05 %, then every k within 0.2 %.
  CGLS: every iteration 1 to 50.
