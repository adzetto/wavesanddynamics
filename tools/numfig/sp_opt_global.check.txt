  in (GA, SA, ACO_R); PSO's walls absorb (the particle stops on the wall, that velocity component
  is set to zero).
  (a) GA: 20 per generation, binary tournament selection (with replacement), BLX-alpha crossover
      (alpha = 0.5, probability 0.9), Gaussian mutation (probability 0.25 per child,
      sigma = 0.6), the 2 best parents replace the 2 worst children: 19 generations.
  (b) PSO: 20 particles, global best, v <- w v + c1 r1 (p - x) + c2 r2 (g - x), w = 0.7298,
      c1 = c2 = 1.49618 (Clerc and Kennedy's constriction, chi = 0.7298 with phi = 4.1); first
      velocities uniform in +-0.8: 19 iterations.
  (c) SA: Gaussian proposals, sigma = 1; Metropolis acceptance, exp(-df / T) for uphill moves;
      T_k = 2 a^k, a = 0.98279358, from 2 to 0.002 over 399 proposals.
  (d) ACO_R (Socha and Dorigo 2008): archive k = 20 solutions sorted by f; rank weights
      w_l = exp(-(l - 1)^2 / (2 q^2 k^2)) / (q k sqrt(2 pi)), q = 0.5; each ant picks one archive
      solution l with probability w_l / sum w and samples N(s_l, sigma_l) in each coordinate,
      sigma_l = xi sum_e |s_e - s_l| / (k - 1), xi = 0.85; m = 2 ants per iteration, then the archive
      keeps the best k: 190 iterations. (The paper's optional rotation of the
      coordinate system for correlated variables is not used.)
